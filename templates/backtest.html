Stock backtester

This runs one of the most basic trading strategies there is, the moving-average crossover, on real historical prices. It's a simple, theoretical model built for learning how backtesting works, not a real trading system. The question it answers: would this strategy have beaten just buying the stock and holding it?

Heads up: this is a deliberately simple, theoretical strategy. Real trading involves fees, taxes, and a lot more nuance, so don't use these results to make actual investment decisions.

What is a moving-average crossover?

A moving average smooths out a stock's price over a set number of days. This strategy watches two of them: a fast one and a slow one. Buy when the fast line crosses above the slow one (the stock is picking up momentum). Sell when it crosses back below (momentum fading). It's one of the oldest and most studied strategies in trading.

It goes all-in or all-out. No partial positions, no borrowing, no trading fees.

This is a simplified, theoretical version meant for learning. A real strategy would account for fees, taxes, slippage, and a lot more.
